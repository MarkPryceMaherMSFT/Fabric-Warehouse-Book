# Appendix K. Ignite 2026 announcements and availability

[Book contents](../index.md) | [Appendices](README.md) | [What's new](../whats-new.md) | [Feature ledger](C-feature-status.md) | [Sources](D-bibliography.md#ignite-review-18-november-2026)

**Availability snapshot: 18 November 2026.** This review covers Tino Tereshko's [Fabric Data Warehouse at Ignite: Built for AI-era analytics][ignite] and the Warehouse and directly related sections of Katie Murray's [November feature summary][november]. Both were **published on 17 November 2026**. An announcement, an invitation to register and an expected delivery month are not proof that a customer can enable a feature today.

The table has **31 editorial coverage rows**, not 31 independent launches. It splits bundled announcements where their availability differs and identifies existing capabilities that Ignite reiterates. No Fabric tenant, preview enrollment or feature activation was tested.

## Read these labels literally

| Source label | What a reader may conclude on 18 November |
| --- | --- |
| **Generally Available / GA** | A GA milestone has been announced for that particular component. Verify its supported route, permissions, region and tenant rollout; the label does not promote adjacent previews to GA. |
| **Preview** | This component is not GA. Use the actual access and support conditions; do not silently expand an unqualified Preview label to Public Preview or imply that it is enabled in every tenant. |
| **Limited Preview / Private Preview / Limited Private Preview** | Restricted evaluation, not a feature ordinary customers can assume they can use now. Registration is not approval. Preserve the exact label; GPU is called Limited Preview, not Limited Private Preview, in the Ignite article. |
| **Coming in November** | A delivery intention within November 2026, **not available-today evidence on 18 November**. Keep it in planning until a released contract and actual access establish otherwise. |
| **Coming soon** | Future functionality, without a usable-today or guaranteed-date claim. Do not invent a setting, API or lab prerequisite. |
| **No individual stage specified** | The roundup establishes an announcement, not a new GA date. Retain the specific documented qualification or record the unresolved scope. |

**Two especially important distinctions:** new metadata sync is **GA announced**, while the new existing-endpoint upgrade path is **Preview**. Warehouse Clones are **Limited Private Preview**, while existing table clones and read-only Warehouse snapshots retain their separate supported contracts.

## How to act without treating a promise as a dependency

For **core new sync**, identify the actual endpoint generation and confirm the applicable new-endpoint behavior. For an **existing-endpoint upgrade**, obtain access to the preview and the operation-specific contract first; assess readiness, approve remediation and the change, track completion and independently verify the data and consumer access. Do not drop a working endpoint to bypass the distinction.

For **clones, GPU acceleration and Live Query Troubleshooting**, record the restricted preview and required enrollment rather than writing a general-customer setup lab. For **cooldown, aliases, graphical plans and new Monitor cards**, describe the design benefit and wait for released controls; neither a calendar month nor a screenshot supplies a supported API.

When references disagree, retain both dates and scopes. At this review the retrieved metadata-sync guide/public source still described the earlier preview/new-endpoints-only path; the monthly announcement supplies the newer GA/default/preview-upgrade distinctions. Native Git/deployment and some data-agent references also retained older qualifications. This book states the announced direction without manufacturing missing implementation details.

The **9 November roadmap snapshot**, **9 November chapter-wide review**, **10 November Build crosswalk**, selected source-code pins and image retrieval dates are preserved.
