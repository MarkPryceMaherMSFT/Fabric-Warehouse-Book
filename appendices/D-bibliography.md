**Original research baseline: 8 October 2026; illustrated revision: 9 October; GPU-paper extension: 10 October; targeted public-documentation refresh: 9 November 2026.** This bibliography is organized by evidence type rather than treating every web page as equally authoritative. Microsoft Learn and public REST references define documented usage; product-team posts establish dated announcements and explain intent; research papers establish architectural lineage and reported experiments; public scripts supply implementations to inspect, not product guarantees. Earlier entries retain their original review context; [the November extension](#revision-2026-11-09) records the newer guidance and rechecks.

Dates below distinguish publication from later updates where verified. A date not independently established is not invented. Microsoft Learn is credited institutionally: its editorial metadata is not used to assign sole technical authorship. Migrated Community bylines are retained as accounts when a full name was not verified. No private telemetry, internal documents, customer information, M365 sources or local customer projects were used.

Older research and explanatory articles remain useful. The **2023-10-08 to 2026-10-08 window applies to script selection**, not the age of research in this bibliography. In particular, the 2020 Polaris paper and July 2023 Managed Instance articles are not counted as qualifying recent code entries.

## Research: architectural lineage and experimental scope

The source-review scope reported here is explicit. The initial bibliography pass verified the public abstracts and bibliographic records of PAPER01–PAPER04, **not the complete full text of all four papers**. Subsequently, the engine-chapter author independently read all 13 pages of both the Polaris 2020 PDF and the Transactions 2024 preprint. Chapters 18, 23 and 25 now cite the relevant architectural passages; those expanded scopes are recorded in PAPER01 and PAPER03. The 10 October GPU research pass reviewed the complete published original TQP, TQP++, TQP demonstration, and scalar-function papers linked in PAPER05–PAPER08. CoddSpeed now has **abstract-plus-award-article** coverage through TEAM12, but its publisher full text remained inaccessible. Unified Query Optimization remains abstract-level. Reading reported experiments is not independent benchmark validation. Detailed claims in Chapter 34 distinguish paper passages from article-reported architecture and measurements; neither abstracts nor related papers establish unreviewed CoddSpeed implementation details.

<a id="paper01"></a>**PAPER01. Aguilar-Saborit, Josep; Ramakrishnan, Raghu; Srinivasan, Krish; Bocksrocker, Kevin; and colleagues.** [POLARIS: the distributed SQL engine in azure synapse](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/polaris-the-distributed-sql-engine-in-azure-synapse/). July 2020. Public Microsoft Research record/abstract; the engine-chapter author also independently reviewed all 13 pages of the [complete public VLDB PDF](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol13/p3204-saborit.pdf). Sections 2-9 establish architectural lineage for compute/state separation, cells, fine-grained scheduling and partial-query recovery; supports Chapters 18, 23 and 25. It describes Synapse-era Polaris, not an exhaustive October 2026 Fabric specification. Section 10's reported one-petabyte TPC-H work was read but not independently reproduced or validated; it is historical experimental evidence, not a service-scale promise.

<a id="paper02"></a>**PAPER02. Bruno, Nicolas; C. Galindo-Legaria; Milind Joshi; Esteban Calvo Vargas; Kabita Mahapatra; Sharon Ravindran; Guoheng Chen; Ernesto Cervantes Juarez; Beysim Sezgin.** [Unified Query Optimization in the Fabric Data Warehouse](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/unified-query-optimization-in-the-fabric-data-warehouse/). *Companion of the 2024 International Conference on Management of Data*, June 2024. Author list and public abstract verified; ACM full-text retrieval returned HTTP 403, so review remains abstract-only. Supports the PDW-to-Fabric optimization narrative in Chapters 18 and 25; does not by itself justify a detailed algorithm or cost-model claim. The more specific unified-frontend compilation discussion uses the reviewed Transactions paper's section 3.3, not an inferred reading of this paper.

<a id="paper03"></a>**PAPER03. Aguilar-Saborit, Josep; Raghu Ramakrishnan; Kevin Bocksrocker; Alan Halverson; and colleagues.** [Extending Polaris to Support Transactions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11162). Preprint submitted 20 January 2024; subsequently SIGMOD 2024, [DOI 10.1145/3626246.3653392](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3626246.3653392). Public abstract establishes the transaction extension, immutable log-structured files and snapshot-isolated multistatement/multitable transactions. Supports Chapters 10, 18, 23 and 25. After the initial abstract-level pass, the engine-chapter author read all 13 pages of the [version-pinned public arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.11162v1), the only arXiv revision at the evidence cutoff. Sections 2-5 support the logical/physical metadata distinction, private manifests, and SQL commit versus asynchronous public Delta publication. Section 3.3 specifically supports unified frontend compilation beyond the 2020 paper's two-phase design. This is the reviewed preprint, not a claim that the inaccessible ACM proceedings PDF was read. Current-product boundaries remain governed by current Fabric documentation.
