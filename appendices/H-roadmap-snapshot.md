## How to use this snapshot without turning it into a promise

When a chapter cites one of these plans, read it as: **"The roadmap snapshot taken on 9 November 2026 listed this target."** Do not rewrite it as "the feature is available," "will ship by this date," or "is enabled for all tenants." Delivery, scope, terminology and timing can change; a planned item can be delayed, changed or removed.

Use the [feature ledger](C-feature-status.md) and current feature/API documentation for today's supported operations. A roadmap can lag shipped documentation: configurable retention and Workspace Identity COPY support illustrate why a Planned label must not force an already documented capability back into a future state. Conversely, an intended GA stage must not erase a current preview qualification.

For a design dependency, record the fallback that works without the planned item, the responsible owner, and the fresh availability check required before adoption. For example, the hybrid Lakehouse/Warehouse pattern works without waiting for Warehouse-native shortcuts. A planned concurrency improvement is not a reason to remove today's writer coordination or retries.

Dates in this table are the site's labels, not date conversions or service commitments. Being within a listed quarter does not establish release. The roadmap's own disclaimer warns that functionality and projected delivery may change or may not be released.

## Refresh policy

Keep this dated observation intact. For a later review, add another clearly dated snapshot or identify a new source revision; do not silently replace the 9 November entries with a moving live view. Reopen individual roadmap items, compare them with release announcements and specific references, and record disagreements.

This appendix captures listing fields and reader implications, not a full implementation specification for each item. It does not claim tenant validation, a product-team commitment, a future price, or access to nonpublic plans.
